Early Mortgage Payoff in Fixed-Rate FHA Loans

Does the gap between a borrower’s fixed rate and Freddie Mac’s benchmark help forecast early payoff?

MySQL · Python / Jupyter · Power BI

Ginnie Mae II loan disclosures. The analysis follows a consistent loan-ID suffix sample of active, current 30-year fixed-rate FHA purchase loans. Each loan’s scheduled maturity falls after its forecast window.

Six-month forecast validation

Across 669,879 scored loan-cohort forecasts from January–March 2024, there were 16,854 observed first payoffs (2.52%).

The rate-gap model’s Brier score, which measures mean squared probability error, was 0.58% lower, a small relative reduction. The paired gap-minus-note difference was −0.000144, with a 95% pool-bootstrap interval of −0.000160 to −0.000129.

Across the three validation cohorts, the share of rows with a loan ID seen in training ranged from 87% to 89%. The gap model had the lower Brier score among 78,490 scored rows with loan IDs absent from training and when payoff took priority over arrears on same-month ties. Both sensitivity checks kept the fitted models fixed.

Data and method

I linked monthly loan disclosures in MySQL. I fitted the models in Python on cohorts starting in December 2017, January 2018 and April–June 2023, then evaluated them on the January–March 2024 cohorts.

Both monthly first-event models use credit score, original loan-to-value, loan age and follow-up month. They differ in the rate input. The rate-gap model subtracts the last weekly 30-year Freddie Mac rate published by the cohort month-end from the borrower’s note rate. The benchmark covers conventional mortgages, and the survey methodology changed in 2022. The gap is a proxy for the borrower’s refinancing incentive.

The models forecast first reported payoff before 60+ day arrears or another removal. A loan ID can contribute forecasts in different cohort months. Both models are scored on the same rows when outcomes are known. Two rows with incomplete follow-up are omitted at six months.

Historical payoff patterns

The January 2018 and July 2023 pilots compared borrowers at least 0.25 percentage points above and below the benchmark. Both groups were weighted to the same mix of credit score, original loan-to-value and loan age bands within each cohort.

The above-minus-below differences in weighted six-month payoff rates were +1.78 percentage points in January 2018 and +3.31 in July 2023. These are descriptive associations. Payoff can follow a refinance or a sale.

Proposed Estonia follow-up

Most new Estonian housing loans in 2025 were tied to six-month Euribor. A local follow-up could test whether savings from a lower offered lender margin, after switching costs, help predict refinancing. Eesti Pank, Financing of the Economy 2026, pp. 26–27
