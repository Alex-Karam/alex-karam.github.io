What the analysis shows

Fewer orders, a higher average charge.

Recorded order charges fell less than order volume because the mean charge per order increased. This describes the combined result; it does not isolate price changes or explain why the order mix changed.

Delivery is the largest recorded remote fulfilment method.

The January–October 2026 remote-order cohort contains 1,690 orders, down 5.00% year on year. Delivery accounts for 59.17%, self-pickup for 38.22%, and an unrecorded method for 2.60%. Unrecorded methods stay in the denominator; displayed shares are rounded.

This cohort uses the report’s seven remote ordering channels. In-shop orders, marketplace orders and orders with an unknown ordering channel are outside that comparison.

Demand comparisons use the fulfilment calendar.

The Demand patterns page compares weekday activity on a consistent January–October basis, excluding confirmed closure dates. Its 2026 headline is 9.95 orders per comparison day. These are fulfilment dates, so the chart describes when orders were due rather than when they were booked.

Method and validation

From spreadsheets to reconciled business reporting.

I ran and reviewed the Excel / Power Query, MySQL and Power BI steps, resolved source ambiguities with the owner, and checked the reporting outputs against agreed definitions.

1. Structure the operational records

Use Power Query to bring monthly worksheets into a consistent structure. Preserve source references and raw values, and distinguish confirmed corrections from unresolved information.

2. Build the SQL reporting layer

Validate source keys and classifications, separate ordering channels from fulfilment methods, and create reporting views for the agreed business questions.

3. Define comparable measures

Connect Power BI to MySQL, set explicit data types, and use a fulfilment-date calendar and DAX measures for counts, charges, means, shares and matched-period changes.

4. Reconcile and test the report

Check report values against SQL controls, test month and chart selections, refresh the model, and review the four exported pages for consistent labels and complete visuals.

Reporting choices: compare the same January–October period in both years, keep unrecorded categories visible, and state the population behind each share. The private validation page supports the checks; the published PDF contains the four business pages.

Scope and interpretation

What these results can support.

  • Order charges are not profit or confirmed cash receipts. The report describes recorded charges. Cost and payment evidence would be needed for other financial conclusions.
  • The time basis is fulfilment. The main comparison uses January–October in each year. It is not a full-year forecast or a measure of booking-date demand.
  • Order counts are not unique customer counts. Contact identifiers and incomplete source descriptions do not establish a fully resolved customer population.
  • Changes are descriptive. A higher mean order charge does not by itself establish a price increase, and channel or weekday differences do not prove their causes.
  • The scope is the four-page report. Product ranking, profitability and forecasting are outside this report. Some identity-dependent questions remain unresolved.
