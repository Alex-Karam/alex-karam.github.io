On 30 June, the €117.00 payout was one day overdue and the €146.00 payout was in transit. The €146.00 payout posted on 1 July as expected. The €117.00 payout posted on 2 July, three calendar days after its expected arrival, clearing the overdue payout exception.

The same four ledger and processor exceptions remained open at both cutoffs, covering a duplicate item, a fee difference, a missing processor record and a refund amount difference.

Method and validation

Tie ledger events to processor items

Match merchant events by ID in MySQL. Retain one copy of duplicate item bt_002 in the payout calculation and sum five canonical processor items into three payouts.

Apply the reporting cutoff

Match eligible bank postings by payout reference and currency through each inclusive UTC cutoff. Compare amounts in integer cents and use expected arrival dates to classify payouts still awaiting deposit.

Validate bank totals and exceptions

The reporting query tied deposits linked to payouts to an independent bank total with a zero-cent difference at both cutoffs. All 42 dbt tests passed on the MySQL reconciliation sources, including checks for exact bank matches and expected exception rows.

Trace the results in Power BI

A shared UTC cutoff slicer filters the payout and exception tables. Exceptions retain source identifiers and CSV line numbers, and both report exports match their SQL totals.

Two-page Power BI report

The first page shows the 30 June cutoff and the second shows 2 July. Each page includes a payout chart, a bank reconciliation table and the exceptions still open at that cutoff.
